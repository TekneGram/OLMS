## I
Urban tree planting can improve city life when programmes are planned carefully and maintained over many years. Trees provide shade during hot weather, reduce surface temperatures, and make busy streets more comfortable for people walking or cycling. Their roots can also absorb rainwater, which may lessen pressure on drainage systems during heavy storms. However, planting campaigns should not focus only on the number of trees installed. City planners need to select species that suit local soil, available space, and changing climate conditions. They must also budget for watering, pruning, and replacement when young trees fail. A successful urban forest requires long-term care, but it can improve public health, strengthen neighbourhoods, and make densely built areas more resilient and pleasant. They can also provide habitat for birds and other small urban wildlife.
